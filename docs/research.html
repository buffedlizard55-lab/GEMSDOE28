Session 10 — what beating 0.3195 costs, in pixels of credit

The gap is a detection gap, not a budget gap. scripts/reachability_frontier.py (evidence/reachability_frontier.json) inverts the official metric. The closed form DTI = TPw / (0.2·TPw·(1−ρ) + 0.2·N + 0.8·|G|) is checked numerically against metric.dti_binary on 23 synthetic grids before use — max absolute residual 2.22e-16, and the substitution FPw = N − MPw holds in every case. At the calibrated |G| = 12,225 px, the best owner-anchored submission earns 4,791 px of credit (39.2% of |G|) at N = 44,090. Reaching 0.3195 at that same budget needs 5,942 px — a gap of +1,151 px, 24.0% more credit than the entire 0.2600 submission captures.

Why thinning cannot buy it

The programme has one exactly-controlled live measurement of marginal efficiency: thinning the same ridge from d=1.5 (60,069 px, 0.2477) to d=2.8 (44,090 px, 0.2600) removed 15,979 dots and lost 495.1 px of credit — 0.03098 credit/px, below the break-even 0.05485 at 0.2600. A dot earning that little does not clear the 0.2·target charge, so no quantity of dots at the current marginal efficiency reaches 0.3195.

Pruning arms are still worth taking — they are free and compose — but each is worth ~0.001–0.010, one to two orders of magnitude short.

What would reach it

New dots that land on genuinely unmapped fault traces, by the credit each earns:

  • e = 0.20 → 8,458 dots
  • e = 0.30 → 4,876 dots
  • e = 0.40 → 3,425 dots
  • e = 0.50 → 2,639 dots

≈2,600–3,400 well-placed dots at 0.4–0.5 credit each. That is an addition arm. Full frontier and |G| sensitivity: evidence/reachability_frontier.json · knowledge/20.

H34: the packing ladder, and the threshold every arm was judged against

State (session 10b, 2026-10-03): gate FAIL, arm CLOSED. The preregistered holdout ran once on fresh seeds 220–229 (40 cells, 4 spatially blocked folds × 10 seeds). Criterion 1 passed — the measured removal efficiency of the 2.828 → 3.000 rung step is 0.01914, below the live break-even 0.05499, with 10/10 seeds and 4/4 folds agreeing. Criterion 2, the direction control, failed: the next rung measures 0.02814, also below the threshold, so the proxy rates every step profitable and cannot locate a stopping rung. Per the frozen protocol: no confirmation, no retuning on 220–229, no candidate TIFF, no weekly slot. The primary download is unchanged.

Why the proxy cannot see the stopping rung

Removal efficiency scales with credit per dot, because e is credit lost divided by mass shed and both scale with how densely an emission covers truth. The proxy detector earns 0.03926 credit per dot; the H19-5 surface earns 0.10867 — 2.77× more. That flattens the proxy's efficiency ladder by 2.3× on the decisive step, so it would keep thinning indefinitely — which is false, since DTI goes to zero at infinite spacing.

The threshold result that outlives the arm

A pixel class with removal efficiency e is worth dropping iff e < τ = 0.2·DTI/(1−0.2·DTI), which rises with DTI. The catalogue-holdout proxy scores 0.0945 → τ = 0.01927. The 0.2600 submission those harnesses inform scores 0.2606 → τ = 0.05499, 2.85× more permissive. Any arm whose efficiency lies in (0.019, 0.055) was rejected by a threshold that does not apply to it.

The rule is confirmed empirically: on 40 independent cells the proxy's ΔDTI crosses zero at measured e = 0.01914 against predicted τ = 0.01927 (ΔDTI -0.000031), and the next step at e = 0.02814 > τ gives ΔDTI -0.002270 — right sign, right magnitude.

A modelling error found and fixed

The new module closed the metric as FP = N − A, charging every emitted pixel full false-positive mass. That is not the metric: it ignores the crowding excess Ã − A, which the inversion measures as 0.18×, 0.43× and 1.46× of A on the three anchors — a factor-of-eight range no fit can absorb. Corrected to FP = (1−γ)N with γ = Ã/N measured at 0.12569 / 0.12576 / 0.12811 — constant to 1.9 %, exactly what non-selective thinning predicts. A second error: the retention curve is measured on a network with 1:1 dot-to-truth density while the surface runs ~10:1, so one fitted density scale rescales the loss.

With L, |G| and γ all measured the model has one free parameter (s = 0.8368) and reproduces three hash-authenticated live scores to ≤ 1.08e-03. The old two-parameter fit is rejected: it fits to 3.2e-04 but recovers |G| = 9,698, 20.7 % below the blind lattice.

What the corrected model says

Best rung is 3.0000 → N = 41,333, DTI 0.26346 against 0.26043 at the shipped rung, i.e. +0.00303 — stable across the whole plausible range of s. And it does satisfy the direction control the proxy missed: e(3.000→3.162) = 0.06592 > τ = 0.05562, so the next rung is correctly judged unprofitable.

This does not reopen the arm. A model projection is not a validated result, and the preregistration is binding. The corrected closure is algebraically identical to the one the reachability frontier above already uses — substituting ρ = γN/A makes the denominators equal term for term — so the two sessions' arithmetic agrees; only the new module had the bug. It is now guarded by tests/test_operating_point.py.

Why it fails, mechanistically

Great Basin hydrothermal discharge is overwhelmingly basin-margin and fault-controlled — which is exactly why those faults are already in the catalogue and why the blended detector already fires along those margins. The base emission has therefore already spent its budget on the structure the springs mark, and the candidate pool (ridge AND active AND NOT base) is left with redundant neighbouring slop that earns almost nothing (0.01320 credit/dot against a not recorded threshold). Radius confirms the reading. At 1 px the arm reaches its best value (0.02095) and at 6 px it degrades to 0.01064: the association is a sub-pixel coincidence that dissolves as the aperture widens, which is the opposite of a geological control. Mean ΔDTI was -0.001805 (control 0.000354).

Scope, stated so the record is not over-read. LOSFO truth is mapped fault geometry held out by system, so the stronger claim — a concealed unmapped permeable structure is marked by a spring — is untested, not refuted. But because LOSFO is an upper bound, failing it is decisive for the arm as constructed: a dot that cannot earn credit against geometry we can see will not earn credit against geometry we cannot. The layer itself is not impugned; only this conjunction as an addition licence.

1. A hard coverage ceiling of ~12%

Only 11.95% of emitted dots (worst fold 9.61%) had a catalogued segment within 1 km whose strike agreed within 20°. The other 88% are neutral by construction and can never be pruned. The 60% coverage precondition existed to catch exactly this. The cause is structural, not tunable: candidate dots are emitted off-catalogue by design, so most have no similarly oriented mapped segment nearby. Any arm that transfers an attribute from mapped faults to off-catalogue candidates inherits that ceiling.

2. The sign of the physics is inverted

The direction control was better than the primary arm, not worse: removing the bottom decile by favourability discarded 0.12855 credit per removed FP while removing the top decile discarded only 0.10104. The dots this score called unfavourably oriented were carrying more DTI credit than the ones it called favourable.

3. Pruning is exhausted as a family

All three arms removed pixels at 0.101–0.140 credit per FP against a break-even inclusion threshold of 0.01929 — the discarded pixels were worth 5–7× break-even. With H31-1 and H32-2 that is three independent pruning arms failing the same way, reaching the same conclusion as the frontier from a completely different direction: at this density, pruning destroys credit whatever physical rationale selects the pixels.

Standing effect. Per the preregistration the result was recorded and nothing else: no retuning, no re-run on a fresh decade, no candidate TIFF, no submission slot.
