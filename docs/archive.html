  1. S26-TCB: Two-sided directional texture-covariance boundary

    Layers: rtp, tmi_hg, cond_surf. Signature: Estimate directional semivariogram/covariance on opposite sides of a candidate boundary after side-specific mean removal; seek a repeatable cross-boundary correlation-length or anisotropy discontinuity rather than a large amplitude edge.

    Missing-fault rationale (hypothesis, not discovery): A fractured or altered damage zone can change spatial texture and directional continuity even when broad magnetic or conductivity amplitudes cancel or the surface scarp is weak. This is a mechanism to test, not an established fault detector.

    Difference from prior implementation: A code inventory found existing gradient/edge-coherence, Hessian/vesselness, texture/context classifiers, and endpoint/graph methods, but no two-sided directional semivariogram or covariance-change operator. It differs from raw edge magnitude and Frangi ridge strength.

    Non-fault mimic: Depositional facies boundaries, intrusive contacts, map-unit changes, survey leveling seams, and raster resampling can also change covariance.. Cost: Medium-high (directional neighborhoods and boundary-side controls; reuse the cached numerical stack).

  2. S26-MCR: Spatially cross-fitted conductivity residual margins

    Layers: cond_surf, depth_to_base_surf, det_elev, det_elev_slope. Signature: Cross-fit a label-free, spatially blocked conditional-quantile model for surface conductivity from basement-depth and terrain context; identify coherent positive/negative residual margins, then test their association with withheld whole-fault segments.

    Missing-fault rationale (hypothesis, not discovery): Fluid alteration or clay-rich damage adjacent to a buried conduit could be locally unusual after accounting for basin/depth context, revealing a fault-related contrast missed by a catalogue emphasizing mapped surface traces. This does not establish that the conductivity-depth layer is basement thickness.

    Difference from prior implementation: The repository contains raw conductivity, clay-cap margins and conductivity interactions, but its source hypothesis inventory reports no cross-fitted conditional-quantile residual operator. This is distinct from multiplying conductivity by dilation or scoring the raw conductivity edge.

    Non-fault mimic: Saline groundwater, lacustrine clay, irrigation return flow, and changing EM investigation depth can create residual conductivity anomalies.. Cost: Medium (spatial cross-fitting and residual diagnostics; reuse the cached numerical stack).

  3. S26-HFR2M: Residual heat-flow and shallow-temperature conjunction

    Layers: USGS Great Basin well heat-flow residuals, GDR #1391 INGENIOUS 2 m temperature probes, competition cond_surf and depth_to_base_surf. Signature: Require a candidate corridor to intersect a spatially supported positive heat-flow residual and an independent 2 m temperature anomaly; do not emit from either thermal channel alone.

    Missing-fault rationale (hypothesis, not discovery): A buried fluid conduit can support shallow thermal expression without a mapped Quaternary scarp. A thermal signal is only corroborating evidence and cannot by itself identify a fault.

    Difference from prior implementation: The repo has separate heat-flow/Euler and well/spring geothermometer/radiometric work; the proposed conjunction requires an official well heat-flow residual plus independent 2 m probes and a candidate corridor, not just one thermal layer.

    Non-fault mimic: Groundwater discharge, warm aquifers, uneven well/probe sampling, and non-tectonic hydrothermal circulation.. Cost: High (external schema, spatial uncertainty, sampling-density controls, and cross-source provenance).

  4. S26-DNT: Drainage-network displacement and knickpoint trains

    Layers: USGS 3DEP 1 m bare-earth DEM tiles, existing LiDAR terrain descriptors for contrast. Signature: Map channel offsets, beheaded channels, wind gaps, and aligned knickpoint trains; only test anomalies where independent lineament evidence predicts a crossing, using spatially blocked holdout segments.

    Missing-fault rationale (hypothesis, not discovery): Drainage geometry can preserve evidence of displacement after low-relief scarps have been diffused or obscured by basin fill; it is not direct evidence of geothermal activity.

    Difference from prior implementation: Prior LiDAR operators primarily score terrain amplitude/roughness/openness/step features. A flow-routing and channel-network topology operator is not present in the current proposal inventory; it must be rechecked against source before implementation.

    Non-fault mimic: Lithologic resistance contrasts, landslides, artificial channels, roads/culverts, and DEM artifacts.. Cost: Very high (large tile acquisition, hydrologic preprocessing, stream-network quality control).
