### 1.1 The DTI Metric PDE and the Marginal Break-Even Theorem

The DOE GEMS Distance-Tolerant Intersection (DTI) metric on a binary prediction set $P \subset \Omega$ against hidden fault truth $G \subset \Omega \setminus \text{Cat}$ is:
$$\text{DTI}(P, G) = \frac{\text{TP}_w(P, G)}{\text{TP}_w(P, G) + \alpha\,\text{FP}_w(P, G) + \beta\,\text{FN}_w(P, G) + \varepsilon}, \quad \alpha = 0.2,\;\beta = 0.8,\;\varepsilon = 10^{-7},$$
where each pixel $x \in \Omega$ receives linear distance-decay credit $K(d) = \max(0, 1 - d / 3\text{ px})$ within a $300\text{ m}$ ($3\text{ px}$) radius:
$$\text{TP}_w(P, G) = \sum_{g \in G} K\big(\min_{p \in P} \|g - p\|_2\big), \qquad \text{FP}_w(P, G) = \sum_{p \in P} \Big[1 - K\big(\min_{g \in G} \|p - g\|_2\big)\Big], \qquad \text{FN}_w(P, G) = |G| - \text{TP}_w(P, G).$$

Differentiating $\text{DTI}$ with respect to a small addition or deletion $(\Delta \text{TP}_w, \Delta \text{FP}_w)$ around an operating point $D_0 = \text{DTI}(P_0, G)$ yields the exact break-even condition (`src/gems27/metric.py:marginal_credit_threshold`):
$$\Delta \text{DTI} > 0 \iff \frac{\Delta \text{TP}_w}{\Delta \text{FP}_w} > \tau(D_0) = \frac{\alpha D_0}{(1 - \beta) D_0 + 1 - D_0} = \frac{0.2 D_0}{1 - 0.2 D_0}.$$
At the four anchored live scores (`registry/live_scores.json`):
- At $D_0 = 0.1223$ (`GEMSDOE26` `dilcond-oof-v1`): $\tau(0.1223) = 0.02507$;
- At $D_0 = 0.2449$ (`GEMSDOE27` `graph-d28-lcc4-b40`): $\tau(0.2449) = 0.05150$;
- At $D_0 = 0.2477$ (`GEMSDOE24` `dotted-h19-5-d1-5`): $\tau(0.2477) = 0.05212$;
- At $D_0 = 0.2600$ (`GEMSDOE25` `dotted-h19-5-d2-8`): $\tau(0.2600) = \mathbf{0.054852}$;
- At $D_0 = 0.3195$ (competition leaderboard #1 `jozef_c`): $\tau(0.3195) = \mathbf{0.068262}$.

This single equation explains the entire 4-point live leaderboard trajectory:
1. **`GEMSDOE24` (`0.2477`, $N = 88{,}789\text{ px}$, $d = 1.5\text{ px}$) $\to$ `GEMSDOE25` (`0.2600`, $N = 45{,}184\text{ px}$, $d = 2.8\text{ px}$):**
   Because $\text{TP}_w$ depends only on the **nearest** predicted pixel $\min_{p \in P}\|g - p\|_2$ within $3\text{ px}$, a Second-Pixel Redundancy Theorem holds along any 1-px fault trace: once one dot sits within $1.4\text{ px}$ of a truth pixel $g$, a second dot $1.5\text{ px}$ away adds $\Delta \text{TP}_w \approx 0$ while every off-fault ridge adds full $\Delta \text{FP}_w = 1$ per dot. Increasing the Poisson-disk spacing from $1.5\text{ px}$ to $2.8\text{ px}$ deleted $43{,}605$ redundant dots along the identical `H19-5` ridge, cutting the $\alpha\,\text{FP}_w$ denominator penalty by $\sim 7{,}850$ units while preserving $>97\%$ of $\text{TP}_w$ (`+0.0123` live DTI).
2. **`GEMSDOE25` (`0.2600`) $\to$ `GEMSDOE27` (`0.2449`, $N = 33{,}154\text{ px}$, `lcc4-b40`):**
   Adding a $d \le 3\text{ px}$ connected-component length filter (`min_size = 4`) deleted $12{,}030$ dots living on short, fragmented $1\text{–}3$-dot segments. In the Great Basin, active geothermal accommodation zones, relay ramps, and en-echelon fault step-overs (`Faulds & Hinz, 2015`) are physically short, discontinuous $200\text{–}400\text{ m}$ segments. Those deleted segments carried marginal efficiency $\sim 0.145\text{ credit/FP} > 2.6\times \tau_{\text{live}}$, costing `-0.0151` live DTI.
3. **`GEMSDOE25` (`0.2600`) $\to$ `GEMSDOE26` (`0.1223`, $N = 121{,}600\text{ px}$, `dilcond-oof-v1`):**
