## Decision context: why the next test must add discoveries

The one-off repository inversion attributes the owner-reported `0.2600` to the `d=2.8` thinning of the H19-5 scarp/geophysics ridge surface: 44,090 dots rather than 60,069 at `d=1.5`, with a larger share of its 300 m distance-weighted credit retained than a uniform 2-D thinning model predicts. The key mechanistic fit is a sparse, approximately one-dimensional trace: at 2.83–3.0 pixel spacing, neighboring predictions still cover the midpoint of a fault trace under the official 300 m triangular kernel, while reducing redundant false-positive mass. The 24-score inversion and implied credit are repository calculations conditioned on owner-reported scores and a blind-lattice truth-size estimate; they do not independently authenticate the score-to-file association.

At the owner-reported `0.2600` operating point, the inversion estimates about 4,791 weighted true-positive pixels. The reported `0.3195` public-best target would require about 5,942 at the same 44,090-dot budget—roughly 1,151 additional credit units (24%). The measured `d=1.5 → d=2.8` marginal retention is only about 0.031 credit per removed dot, below the roughly 0.055 live break-even. Thus another rearrangement or pruning of the same ridge family is not a credible route to the target. The remaining route is new, high-specificity off-catalogue fault evidence.

The ranking is risk-adjusted for expected incremental DTI per engineering cost and availability, not sorted only by the most optimistic ceiling. All expected ranges include zero; the evidence history makes zero or negative outcomes plausible.

### H38-1 — exact layers, signature, novelty boundary, and risk

- **Layers:** `evidence/h31_1_euler_clusters.csv` (SI-0 Euler source solutions from training `tmi`, with fixed `depth_mad_m ≤ 60`, `median_depth_m ≤ 400`, and `n_solutions ≥ 8` criteria); competition-mirror training band 18 `iso_grav_anom_hg`; owner-mirror LiDAR descriptor band 9 `relief` and band 12 `valid`; the catalogue raster only for exclusion/leakage controls.
- **Physical signature:** a depth-coherent, shallow, contact-like magnetic Euler cluster is corroborated by a nearby strong *independently measured* gravity gradient and subdued local relief. This is a candidate buried density/magnetic contact beneath a surface that lacks a strong scarp—not proof that the source is a fault, active, permeable, or geothermal.
- **Why it could find faults missing from the public catalogue:** the USGS Quaternary Fault and Fold Database is a compilation of mapped structures with geologic evidence of surface deformation; DrivenData itself states that known-fault ground truth is incomplete and that experts manually identified additional faults not in the public USGS set. A buried or subdued structure can lack a mappable surface trace while still generating subsurface potential-field contrasts. The low-relief condition is intended to target that gap rather than simply rediscovering high-relief catalogued scarps.
- **How it differs from repository work:** H37-3 used shallow SI-0 Euler depth coherence alone as an emission licence and was real but not promotable: `+0.000576` mean far-field ΔDTI, 13/20 positive cells, and `0.031157` credit per dot versus the `0.0548` live break-even. H37-2 proposed potential-field lineaments × low-relief terrain but has not been run. H38-1 is a new, narrower conjunction that adds the Euler source-depth estimate to that cross-field/terrain test; it does not reuse H37-3’s Euler-only rule or its thresholds as an outcome-tuned search.
- **Data-only sufficiency check (not a validation):** using the fixed H37-3 Euler cluster criteria, band 18 values at or above the in-footprint 80th percentile, a ≤200 m distance to that gravity-gradient mask, and LiDAR `relief` at or below the valid-footprint median produces **140 unique candidate clusters** from 1,435 H37-3 candidates (9.76%). The thresholds/count are label-free; no LOSFO split, holdout score, seed, or leaderboard value was read to select them. The exact quantiles and hashes are written to `evidence/h38_1_sufficiency_audit.json` after the candidate builder is frozen.
- **Confounders:** Euler SI=0 is an idealized contact geometry, not a fault classifier; the repository records large SI sensitivity and unauthenticated magnetic units. Gravity edges can be lithologic contacts or basin margins. The LiDAR `relief` layer is a quantized, local descriptor derived from an owner-mirrored product, not absolute elevation. The conjunction may preferentially select basin boundaries and still miss fault geometry. H37-3’s 1.65×-random signal was below the live-value threshold, so a positive LOSFO ΔDTI alone will not pass the promotion gate.

### H38-2 — thermal residual (queued, not yet runnable)

Use the USGS Great Basin heat-flow well residual (measured value minus estimated de-convected background) from the 2022 ScienceBase release (DOI `10.5066/P9BZPVUC`), the GDR #1391 2 m temperature probes, and an independent potential-field corridor. The target is a spatially supported thermal anomaly aligned with a structural edge, not simply a spring or warm well. This is distinct from H35-1, whose current spring/well discharge-point conjunction failed its far-field gate below the live bar and below a matched random control. H33-3/H35-2 are earlier, still-untested versions of the residual/probe idea; this record does not claim the broad concept is new. Required before any holdout: fetch and pin the official archive, inspect the residual/probe schema and footprint coverage, and freeze a single conjunction/rate rule. The ScienceBase item lists the 124.12 MB archive and residual attribute; the GDR page lists a downloadable probe archive under its CC BY 4.0 terms. Listing is not proof of a local clip or coverage.

### H38-3 — paleogeothermal deposits (queued, not yet runnable)

Use the GDR #1391 paleogeothermal features (sinter, tufa/travertine and other mapped spring-deposit features) with a separate gravity/magnetic contact corridor and low-relief context. The physical target is an old or partly buried fluid pathway expressed by a spring deposit but not represented as a current Quaternary surface trace. This differs from H35-1’s present-temperature/well point process; it is not a licence to emit around every site. GDR lists the 82 kB resource and CC BY 4.0 terms, and a prior runner inventory recorded byte availability, but the feature file is not locally clipped/audited in this checkout. Sparse and spatially biased exposure is the main risk.

### H38-4 — drainage-network neotectonics (high-cost, data gate open only at product level)

Use the USGS 3DEP 1 m bare-earth DEM to derive channel networks and test for channel offsets, aligned knickpoint trains, beheaded streams, or wind gaps that cross an independently derived potential-field lineament. These are geomorphic displacement signatures, not the amplitude/roughness/curvature features already represented in the LiDAR descriptor raster. They may record deformation where a scarp has been softened or buried. The official USGS catalog describes the one-meter DEM collection as lidar-derived, bare-earth, and public domain. The local `dem_links.json` is an owner-mirrored list of 716 URLs; neither the complete URLs nor actual coverage/bytes were independently validated here. Therefore this idea is not yet marked runnable, and its high ceiling is not a data-availability claim.

### H38-5 — multi-depth MT conductance (coarse-context hypothesis)

Use USGS conductance layers at 2–12, 12–20, 20–50, 50–90 and 90–200 km depth with an independent gravity/magnetic contact corridor. The hypothesized signature is a shallow upper-crustal conductive boundary/corridor that aligns with a candidate structure while the deeper column differs, rather than a blanket conductance or strain threshold. It may mark concealed fluid/alteration structure absent from a surface-fault compilation. This differs from the live-refuted `dilcond` feature (single `cond_surf × geod_dilaterate`) and H32-2’s magnetic spectral-depth screen. The ScienceBase release lists the five layers and their depths; the pixel support is regional/coarse relative to 100 m fault traces, so it ranks last. All five byte hashes, grids and in-footprint coverage must be verified before use.
