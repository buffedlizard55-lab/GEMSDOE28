The exact operators were described as untried in earlier G28 reviews; the incomplete current registry prevents asserting that no parallel lane has since tried them.

1. **Displaced potential-field piercing markers** — paired RTP/TMI/gravity marker offsets across a corridor. A lithologic branch or survey seam can mimic a fault. Medium-high cost; [USGS GeoDAWN](https://www.sciencebase.gov/catalog/item/657e1d85d34e23d3533209f7).
2. **Lithology-conditioned U/Th transport dipoles** — ordered radiometric residuals, rather than a scalar ratio. Groundwater redox fronts and sorting are mimics; medium cost, uncertain physics and raw units. [USGS GeoDAWN](https://www.sciencebase.gov/catalog/item/657e1d85d34e23d3533209f7).
3. **Paired-geothermometer cooling corridors** — directional contrasts in quality-controlled well/spring measurements rather than a circular temperature halo. Mixing, production and biased sampling mimic a corridor; [DOE GDR INGENIOUS](https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1391). External coverage, licensing and leakage risks need checking.
A fourth proposed opposite-flank curvature arm was withdrawn after concurrent PR #57 tested a related signed-piedmont curvature method; do not call that family untried without a full registry audit.

These sources motivate investigation, not claims that a detector works. [Faulds and Hinz (2015)](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1724082) discuss geothermal associations with fault steps, tips and intersections, but a geothermal association does not prove hidden-fault prediction.
