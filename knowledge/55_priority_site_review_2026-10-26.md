### Why GEMSDOE32 appears to lead its site family

The byte-level result supports **pruning/emission hygiene**, not a novel detector: compared with the solo-r1 file, GEMSDOE32 removes a 2,545-dot ring close to the visible-fault catalogue and adds nothing. In a Tversky-style objective, suppressing predictions with little withheld-truth credit can reduce the false-positive term while retaining true-positive credit. This is a plausible mechanism for a higher score, but because the claimed 0.2778 is not tied to an organizer receipt and private labels are unavailable, **we cannot prove the removed dots were false positives or that pruning caused that score**. Recreating that pruning is also a duplicate, not a new strategy.

## Four new, untested hypotheses ranked for a future preregistration

These are distinct candidate mechanisms from the closed S18 migration/relocation tests. The ranks are qualitative expected information value, not predicted scores. No candidate was run or fitted in this review.

1. **Displaced potential-field piercing markers — highest qualitative priority; medium-high cost.** Layers: cached RTP/TMI and isostatic-gravity anomalies, with LiDAR strike/validity as a support mask. Signature: register paired marker ridges on opposite sides of a corridor; demand at least two coherent lateral offsets with consistent direction and score the intervening corridor rather than the contacts. A fault may displace pre-existing contacts without a mapped Quaternary scarp. Distinct from Euler depth estimation, upward-continuation ridge migration, ordinary edge co-location and catalogue-gap closure. Mimics: intrusive cross-cutting contacts, branching lithologic contacts, survey-registration seams. Existing cache was hash-verified in the prior S18 run; new external data are not needed for an initial test, but source authenticity remains distinct from mirror integrity. [USGS GeoDAWN release](https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and).
2. **Lithology-conditioned U/Th transport dipoles — moderate qualitative priority; medium cost.** Layers: GeoDAWN K, Th, U and U/Th, plus gravity-edge orientation. Signature: odd-symmetric cross-corridor U/Th residual conditioned on local Th and K, persisting along strike, with missingness and quantization canaries. Concealed fluid/redox transport could occur without a preserved scarp. Distinct from scalar ratios, ridge conjunctions and prior magnetic trough/Th-K depletion. Mimics: groundwater redox fronts, sediment sorting, radon disequilibrium, airborne artifacts. The official GeoDAWN inventory describes radiometric products, but original binary provenance has not been re-authenticated in this review. [USGS GeoDAWN release](https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and).
3. **Paired-geothermometer cooling corridors — low-medium qualitative priority; medium cost and data gate.** Layers: GDR #1391 well/spring site coordinates, measured temperature, paired quartz/chalcedony estimates, and magnetic/gravity geometry. Signature: after site deduplication, persistent directional difference between reservoir-temperature estimates and measured temperatures along a multi-site corridor, not a circular hot-spring buffer. A connected discharge path may not coincide with a mapped surface rupture. Distinct from max-geothermometer/near-site threshold methods. Mimics: groundwater mixing, incomplete equilibration, drilling/production effects and sampling bias. The official GDR listing is accessible and links the archive, but archive bytes/schema/footprint/licence transformations were not re-authenticated here; this is not yet data-ready for a holdout. [DOE GDR INGENIOUS #1391](https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1391).
4. **Opposite-flank flexural curvature pairs — lowest qualitative priority; medium cost.** Layers: detrended elevation, LiDAR upface/downface/relief/strike/validity, and long-wavelength gravity. Signature: ordered signed broad-curvature lobes on opposite sides of a corridor plus consistent relief asymmetry. Distributed flexure can occur above a buried fault without a sharp mapped scarp. Distinct from gradient strength and unsigned curvature magnitude. Mimics: alluvial-fan margins, paleoshorelines, compaction, lithology-controlled erosion and raster seams. Coarse screening uses existing cached descriptors; high-resolution confirmation needs USGS 3DEP tiles, whose coverage/bytes were not checked in this review, so a high-resolution experiment is not considered viable yet. [USGS 3DEP catalog](https://data.usgs.gov/datacatalog/data/USGS:77ae0551-c61e-4979-aedd-d797abdcde0e).
