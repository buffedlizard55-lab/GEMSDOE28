Overview / Nine-site review

Why the cited GEMSDOE32 result may have risen

The local cross-site byte audit reports that H33-2-B2 is a pruned version of an existing solo-r1 raster, removing 2,545 catalogue-flank dots and adding none. The underlying raster and full original process are not part of the present working checkout, so this page relies on the recorded audit rather than a fresh byte comparison.

Under a proximity-tolerant metric, predictions close to already represented structures can be redundant: removing a prediction may lower false-positive cost while leaving nearby predictions able to earn spatial credit. That is a plausible reason a sparse subset can outperform a denser map. It is not certain: pruning can also discard a true hidden fault, and catalogue distance can leak if it is not recomputed from visible faults inside each buffered holdout. The owner's score claim itself lacks a receipt here, so causation is not established.

Answer: the cited 0.2778 cannot yet be explained as an organizer-confirmed result. Beating it is possible in principle but unproven. The current evidence does not justify claiming a higher score or uploading a derivative.

Rank 1 · Highest qualitative information potential

Displaced potential-field piercing markers

Layers/signature: reduced-to-pole/TMI and isostatic-gravity grids; coherent lateral offsets of multiple marker ridges across a corridor.

Missing-fault rationale: a buried fault may displace magnetic/density contacts without a mapped surface scarp. Score the offset corridor, not the contact itself.

Non-fault mimic: intrusive or branching lithologic contacts and survey-registration seams.

Difference/cost: patch-registration/displacement differs from ordinary edge-coherence, Euler depth, and catalogue gap closure. Medium-high; local cached features are currently absent.

Rank 2 · Moderate, physically ambiguous

Lithology-conditioned U/Th transport dipoles

Layers/signature: GeoDAWN uranium, thorium and potassium; signed odd-symmetric U/Th residuals conditioned on local Th/K.

Missing-fault rationale: fluid/redox transport may modify mobile uranium across concealed permeable structures.

Non-fault mimic: groundwater redox fronts, sediment sorting, radon disequilibrium, or survey artifacts.

Difference/cost: tests dipole morphology, not a scalar ratio threshold. Medium; input bytes are absent locally and quantized ranks cannot provide absolute geochemistry.

Rank 3 · Low-moderate, sparse observations

Paired-geothermometer cooling corridors

Layers/signature: deduplicated GDR well/spring locations, measured temperature, and paired quartz/chalcedony geothermometers; directional multi-site contrasts.

Missing-fault rationale: connected thermal discharge may follow an unmapped permeable fault without a surface rupture.

Non-fault mimic: groundwater mixing, geothermometer disequilibrium, drilling/production, or preferential sampling.

Difference/cost: preserves paired contrasts rather than max-temperature buffers. Medium; source listing does not establish local raw-data availability.

Rank 4 · Lowest in this slate

Opposite-flank flexural curvature pairs

Layers/signature: detrended elevation, LiDAR relief/upface/downface, validity, and broad gravity context; ordered signed curvature lobes on opposite corridor flanks.

Missing-fault rationale: distributed flexure could reveal a buried structure without a sharp scarp.

Non-fault mimic: alluvial fans, paleoshorelines, differential compaction, lithology-controlled erosion, or data seams.

Difference/cost: ordered paired response, not scalar curvature magnitude. Medium to high; USGS 3DEP coverage and bytes require verification.
